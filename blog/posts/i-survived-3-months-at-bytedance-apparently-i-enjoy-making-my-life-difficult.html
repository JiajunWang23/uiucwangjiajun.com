I survived 3 months at ByteDance 😭 Apparently I enjoy making my life difficult.

Here is everything I learned:
𝟭. 𝗜 𝗹𝗲𝗮𝗿𝗻𝗲𝗱 𝘁𝗵𝗲 𝗯𝗲𝘀𝘁 𝘄𝗮𝘆 𝘁𝗼 𝗴𝗲𝘁 𝗶𝗻
Recruiter reachout > team connection > referral > cold application.
Yes, clicking “Apply” is somehow the hardest mode.
𝟮. 𝗜 𝗹𝗲𝗮𝗿𝗻𝗲𝗱 𝘁𝗵𝗮𝘁 𝗮 𝗴𝗼𝗼𝗱 𝗿𝗲𝗳𝗲𝗿𝗿𝗲𝗿 𝗶𝘀 𝗯𝗮𝘀𝗶𝗰𝗮𝗹𝗹𝘆 𝗮 𝘀𝗲𝗰𝗿𝗲𝘁 𝘄𝗲𝗮𝗽𝗼𝗻
Find someone who actually knows you.
They may be able to see your progress, follow up with HR, and help you understand what happened.
This works in both China and the US.
𝟯. 𝗜 𝘀𝘁𝗼𝗽𝗽𝗲𝗱 𝗯𝗲𝗹𝗶𝗲𝘃𝗶𝗻𝗴 𝗶𝗻 𝟳 𝗣𝗠
If you work in R&D, 9 PM starts looking suspiciously normal.
𝟰. 𝗜 𝗱𝗶𝘀𝗰𝗼𝘃𝗲𝗿𝗲𝗱 𝘁𝗵𝗮𝘁 𝗴𝗼𝗼𝗱 𝗽𝗮𝘆 𝗮𝗻𝗱 𝘀𝘂𝗳𝗳𝗲𝗿𝗶𝗻𝗴 𝗰𝗮𝗻 𝗰𝗼𝗲𝘅𝗶𝘀𝘁
Interns can make around RMB 500/day.
The compensation is real.
So is the exhaustion.
𝟱. 𝗜 𝗯𝗲𝗰𝗮𝗺𝗲 𝘃𝗲𝗿𝘆 𝗴𝗼𝗼𝗱 𝗮𝘁 𝘀𝗶𝘁𝘁𝗶𝗻𝗴
Meetings through dinner.
Legs going numb.
Ordering the most expensive ride home at 9:30 PM…
…and still not getting a car.
Peak productivity.
𝟲. 𝗜 𝗹𝗲𝗮𝗿𝗻𝗲𝗱 “𝗜 𝗰𝗮𝗻’𝘁 𝗱𝗼 𝗶𝘁” 𝗶𝘀 𝗻𝗼𝘁 𝗮𝗻 𝗮𝗻𝘀𝘄𝗲𝗿
If something cannot be done, you still need to bring:
a reason,
an alternative,
and a solution.
No matter how crazy the requirement sounds :)
𝟳. 𝗜 𝗴𝗼𝘁 𝘁𝗼 𝘁𝗼𝘂𝗰𝗵 𝗽𝗿𝗼𝗱𝘂𝗰𝘁𝗶𝗼𝗻
Very exciting.
Until something breaks.
Then suddenly you understand why testing, monitoring, rollback, and ownership exist.
𝟴. 𝗜 𝗹𝗲𝗮𝗿𝗻𝗲𝗱 𝘁𝗵𝗮𝘁 𝗻𝗼 𝗵𝗲𝗮𝗱𝗰𝗼𝘂𝗻𝘁 𝗺𝗲𝗮𝗻𝘀… 𝗻𝗼 𝗵𝗲𝗮𝗱𝗰𝗼𝘂𝗻𝘁
You can perform well.
Your manager can like you.
Your team can like you.
But if there is no HC…
Even the CEO probably cannot invent one for you 😭
𝟵. 𝗜 𝗹𝗲𝗮𝗿𝗻𝗲𝗱 𝘁𝗼 𝗱𝗼𝗰𝘂𝗺𝗲𝗻𝘁 𝗺𝘆 𝗶𝗺𝗽𝗮𝗰𝘁
Don’t wait until your final presentation to remember what you did.
Write down the metrics.
Write down the ownership.
Write down the problems you solved.
Your work does not speak for itself nearly as loudly as you think.
𝟭𝟬. 𝗜 𝗹𝗲𝗮𝗿𝗻𝗲𝗱 𝘁𝗵𝗮𝘁 𝗰𝗿𝗼𝘀𝘀-𝘁𝗲𝗮𝗺 𝗰𝗼𝗻𝘃𝗲𝗿𝘀𝗶𝗼𝗻 𝗶𝘀 𝗛𝗮𝗿𝗱 𝗠𝗼𝗱𝗲
If your team may not have HC, start networking early.
“Early” does not mean three days before your internship ends.
𝟭𝟭. 𝗜 𝗳𝗶𝗻𝗮𝗹𝗹𝘆 𝗿𝗲𝗺𝗲𝗺𝗯𝗲𝗿𝗲𝗱 𝘁𝗵𝗮𝘁 𝗺𝘆 𝗯𝗼𝗱𝘆 𝗶𝘀 𝗺𝗶𝗻𝗲
Jobs disappear.
Teams change.
Headcount changes.
Your career will survive one rejection, one bad project, or one missing return offer.
Your health is much harder to replace.

So yes.
Three months later:
Still alive.
Still building.
Still learning.
And slightly more respectful of sleep. 😂
