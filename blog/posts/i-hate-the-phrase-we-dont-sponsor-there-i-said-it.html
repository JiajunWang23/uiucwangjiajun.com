I Hate the Phrase “We Don’t Sponsor.” There, I Said It.

Another application.
Another rejection.

Not because of my experience.
Not because I failed an interview.
Not because someone else was a stronger candidate.

Because of one sentence:
“We are only able to consider candidates who are authorized to work in the U.S. and do not require sponsorship.”

I know.
Companies have budgets.
Immigration lawyers cost money.
Policies come from HR, legal, finance, or leadership.

Not every company wants to deal with the uncertainty of immigration.
I understand the business reasoning.
I still hate it.

Because behind the checkbox is a person.
A person who studied here.
Worked here.
Paid taxes here.
Built production systems here.
Interned at companies here.
And is currently legally authorized to work here.

Yet sometimes the conversation ends before anyone even looks at what I can build.
The frustrating part is that international students already know this question is coming.

“Will you now or in the future require sponsorship?”
And sometimes you know that clicking “Yes” means your application may never reach a human being.

No technical interview.
No behavioral interview.
No opportunity to explain yourself.

Just:
Yes → Rejected.
I don't expect every company to sponsor me.
But I wish more companies saw sponsorship as an investment in talent instead of automatically treating it as a liability.

Because I don't want special treatment.
I just want the chance to compete.

Judge my code.
Judge my projects.
Judge my internships.
Judge my interviews.
Reject me because someone was better.
That I can accept.

But getting rejected before I even get the chance to introduce myself?
Yeah.
I'll probably always hate that.
