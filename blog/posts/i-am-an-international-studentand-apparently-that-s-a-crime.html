Every recruiting season, I notice the same pattern.

A recruiter reaches out.
They tell me my background is a strong fit.
They encourage me to apply.
Sometimes we even have a great conversation.

Then comes a simple question:
"Do you require sponsorship?"
The moment I answer "yes," the conversation often ends.

No update.
No feedback.
Sometimes, not even a reply.

I understand that companies face real constraints. Sponsorship involves legal processes, costs, and uncertainty. This post is not meant to criticize recruiters or employers for making business decisions.

But as an international student, it is difficult not to feel frustrated.
We pay tuition that is often two or three times higher than what domestic students pay.

We leave our families, cultures, and support systems behind.
We cross oceans to pursue an education in the United States.
We spend years studying, conducting research, building projects, and interning at leading companies.

Yet sometimes it feels as though all of that can be outweighed by a single checkbox.
The most discouraging part is not rejection.
Rejection is a normal part of life.
The discouraging part is feeling disqualified before your skills, experience, and potential are even considered.
I don't believe this is primarily a company problem.

I believe it is a policy problem.
A system should not actively recruit international students, benefit from their tuition, research contributions, and talent, and then make it extraordinarily difficult for many of them to participate in the workforce after graduation.
International students are not asking for special treatment.
We are asking for the opportunity to compete on merit.
We came here to learn, contribute, innovate, and build careers—not to be treated as temporary revenue sources.

I'm curious:
For other international students, have you experienced the same thing?
For recruiters and hiring managers, how do you think the system could be improved?
